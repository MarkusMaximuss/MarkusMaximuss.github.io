I attended another lecture by Medio Modo on the topic "How the Russian Language Didn't Switch to the Latin Alphabet." It turns out that in 1929, the Russian written language in the USSR was planned to be converted to the Latin alphabet. It was believed that a global revolution would soon occur and all nations would need to be united through a single script. Later, the idea of ​​a global revolution was abandoned, replaced by the idea of ​​building socialism in a single country. Therefore, they decided to strengthen the role of the Russian language and its main attribute – the Cyrillic alphabet. You can read about this project in the [Wikipedia article](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A0%D1%83%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%8F_%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0_%D0%AF%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B0 ).
Если бы русский язык все-таки перевели на латиницу, эта знаменитая фраза Кота Бегемота из «Мастер и Маргарита» выглядела бы так:
Ne şalü, nikogo ne trogaju, pocinäju primus, i jescö scitaju dolgom predupreditj, cto kot drevneje i neprikosnovennoje ƶivotnoje.
